## Purpose 
Pursuing an Electrical Engineering career requires intentional professional development to build real-world skills employers look for. This graduation learning plan establishes best practices for Electrical Engineering students at UW Bothell to be able to gain hands-on technical experience, industry exposure, and professional skills beyond the classroom. Following these best practices prepares you to successfully apply for entry-level Electrical Design Engineer positions after graduation. 

## Mastering circuit design through hands-on projects
Working as an electrical design engineer requires real hands- on experience building circuits and laying out printed circuit boards, more than just memorizing formulas [7, 10]. Employers look for students who have built hardware, reverse-engineered devices, and have experience with budgeting components. Taking apart electronics and building them back helps you map out power networks in low-risk environments. Then managing part numbers on websites like DigiKey helps you build experience navigating real-world supply chain practices. 

Build your own independent electronics projects utilizing breadboards, benchtop instruments, or make custom PCB designs in different CAD software such as KiCad or Autodesk. Practice working under a specific budget and goals; document your progress, note what went well and what challenged you. Then, when you finish a project, post your design, your findings, and what you learned on platforms such as GitHub and LinkedIn.  

## Utilizing open-source tools and industry standards
Taking advantage of free software, parent databases, and open-sources libraries helps you to learn professional standards without having to spend a lot of money. 
Electrical design teams often rely on vendor schematics and open-source designs to help speed up development. As a student, you have access to student discounts and  UWB library access, which gives you access to IEEE Xplore, ANSI, and ISO standards, which will help you learn how real products are designed and regulated. Citing these official sources on your projects shows you are already thinking like an industry professional.

As a student, software like MATLAB, LTspice, and CAD design tools are free when you use your UW email. Research patents from local companies on Google Patents and find application notes from chip designers like Texas Instruments to see how engineers lay out their boards. Practice writing code in Python to automate things in CAD, and again share your code and files on platforms like GitHub.

## Gaining practical experience on campus
Building a strong background in electrical design can start right here on campus through tech clubs, campus jobs, and student competition events. Joining campus groups such as TrickFire Robotics, UWB ACM, and the UWB Electronics Club gives you direct experience designing circuit boards, telemetry systems, and power distribution boards. Working in campus lab spaces gives you constant practice using testing gear and troubleshooting hardware issues. Being a part of campus clubs and organizations will also increase your network, as most clubs have connections with different industry organizations and resources.

Moreover, participating in campus events such as hackathons, such as the annual one held by ACM and ACMW in partnership with other clubs, and participating in campus design challenges will build your practical skills. Through these events, you will get a chance to develop your software, CAD, and hardware design skills in a team setting. Utilize these events to seek constructive feedback from lab techs, student organization officers, and team members to refine your circuit layout and debugging skills. 

## Building professional networks through local organizations
Finding an electrical engineering job depends heavily on networking, clear communication, and getting involved with local tech groups outside of class. Look beyond giant tech companies such as Boeing and SpaceX; targeting mid-sized contract manufacturers or aerospace suppliers in cities around you will give you access to entry-level roles with less competition. Getting involved with the IEEE Seattle Section, the Emerging Leaders in Engineering program, and Career Services events helps to connect you with working engineers and hiring managers. Reaching out to alumni through Husky Landing lets you build genuine connections with former UW students.

Attend IEEE Seattle Section monthly meetings to meet practicing engineers, ask them about their daily work, and set up informational interviews over coffee or Zoom. Look into local electronics manufacturing services suppliers in Bothell for upcoming internship openings. Utilize Handshake to attend employer events targeting Northwest companies like Blue Origin, Gene, and Crane. Connect with former UW electrical engineering graduates on Husky Landing to get direct advice on your resume and interview preparation.
